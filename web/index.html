    Underwater colour correction for photos and videos

    Water absorbs red light first, then orange and yellow, so underwater photos and videos come out blue or green. Reefhue measures the water colour in each photo, or in every frame of a video, and brings those colours back automatically. Sliders let you fine-tune the result.

    It is free, needs no account, and runs entirely in your browser: your photos and videos are never uploaded. Reefhue is open source: see the code on GitHub.

    Supported files

    Photos: JPEG, HEIC, TIFF, PNG, WebP and camera RAW files (ARW, CR2, CR3, NEF, DNG, ORF, RW2, RAF and more, using the preview the camera stores in the file).
    Videos: MP4, MOV, MTS and M2TS (AVCHD), MKV and WebM, from action cameras, phones, drones and dive cameras.

    Photos are saved at full resolution as JPEG, PNG or WebP. Videos are saved as MP4 or WebM, with the original sound kept.

    Questions

    Is Reefhue free?

    Yes. There is no sign-up, no watermark and no limit on the number of files.

    Are my photos uploaded anywhere?

    No. Everything is processed on your phone or computer, in the browser. Nothing is sent to a server.

    Does it work with GoPro and phone videos?

    Yes. Open the MP4 or MOV file straight from the camera or the phone gallery. Auto follows the water colour as the scene changes, or you can keep one frame’s correction for the whole clip.

    Can I correct many photos at once?

    Yes. Add several files to make a batch. Each file uses Auto unless you change its settings. Then process them all and download them as one zip file.

    Why do underwater photos look blue or green?

    Water filters out red light within a few metres, and orange and yellow a little deeper. The deeper and further away the subject, the less of those colours reach the camera. Green water adds plankton and algae to the mix. Reefhue estimates how much of each colour is missing and restores it.
